### Ghost-sample finding

The 1.2.2 changelog said that a tracker returning peers for a ghost info-hash was removed from published feeds. That behavior was never connected: `observe_sample` records `poisoned_peers` for the `sample_announce` stage, while feed publication looked for it among health `announce` events.

A public API measurement on 2026-11-16 (approximately 13:40–14:10 UTC) queried all 309 listed endpoints for `stage=sample_announce`, `window=24h`; 16,928 rows were returned. Sixty-three endpoints (20%) had at least one failed result with a null public `error_category`, 290 rows total. This consisted of 36 of 157 UDP, 21 of 83 HTTP and 6 of 69 HTTPS endpoints. Those endpoints included 33 of 51 `trackers.txt` entries, 9 of 20 `trackers_best.txt` entries and 51 of 92 `trackers_all.txt` entries.

The later private-database aggregate confirms the diagnosis that the public API's missing-category rows reflected the ghost-sample `poisoned_peers` verdict; it is a rolling-window aggregate, not a row-for-row reconciliation of the public query. For the rolling 24-hour window ending 2026-11-17T02:49:44Z, there were 19,081 sample records, of which 998 were `poisoned_peers`, involving 70 endpoints and 16 distinct ghost samples. All 998 records came from the ghost-sample source. The first match was 2026-11-16T07:39:11Z. Sixty-seven endpoints hit four or more distinct samples, three hit two or three, and none hit only one. The private aggregate and earlier public API query have different observation times and scopes; their counts are not interchangeable.

The cause of the broad firing remains unestablished. One unverified possibility is that a tracker returns the observatory's own registration; ghost hashes are also derived from a string in this public repository, so a third party can register peers on them. The earlier public API snapshot showed this pattern on 63 of 309 listed endpoints, including 33 of 51 standard-feed entries; connecting the exclusion as written would therefore have affected roughly two thirds of that feed. The later private aggregate confirms the verdict category, but is not a row-for-row mapping of the earlier API window.

Requiring repeated hits across distinct ghost samples is not a useful discriminator: the private aggregate shows repeated hits for all 70 endpoints, with 67 hitting at least four samples. Two other possible approaches remain unverified: exclude the observatory's own addresses, or require a completed handshake with a returned peer. Neither is implemented or established as reliable by this release.
